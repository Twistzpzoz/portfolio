Case study 04Product design · UI/UX & AI

At Chris

From chat chaos to one clear system. Online ordering for a neighbourhood Burmese rice shop, redesigned end to end for customers, the owner and drivers.

My role
Core team member across research, design & build
Team
Team CodingRats · 3 members · advisor A. Jinchan Lu
Context
Senior project, Assumption University
Built with
Next.js, Supabase, Gemini, Google Maps
§ 01

The problem

At Chris took every order through LINE chat. That worked until lunch got busy. We found six gaps in one broken journey:

  1. No visual menu: no way to browse what's available today.
  2. Language barrier: the menu existed only in Burmese.
  3. Manual tracking: orders arrived as unstructured chat messages.
  4. No confirmation: customers never knew an order was received.
  5. No delivery view: no visibility once food left the shop.
  6. No sales data: no record of trends, popular dishes or peak hours.
§ 02

My role

At Chris was our three-person Senior Project 2, built under the guidance of our advisor. As a team we designed and built the whole system: the customer ordering flow, the owner's admin dashboard, the driver app and the AI features.

On a three-person team there was no room for narrow roles. I worked on almost every part of the project, from the first conversations about the problem to the live system:

  • Research: mapping how the shop worked over LINE and the six gaps it created.
  • Design: the customer ordering flow, the trilingual menu and the screens for customers, the owner and drivers.
  • Build: the customer app, admin dashboard and driver app, and the AI features for sales insights and payment-slip checks.
  • Delivery: testing, deployment, the project deck and presenting.
§ 03

The process

  1. Design the customer flow

    Seven taps from hungry to happy: browse, pick 2–4 dishes (฿50, ฿65 or ฿75), enter name and room, scan a PromptPay QR, have the slip verified, track delivery, receive food. No app download; it runs in any phone browser.

  2. Make the menu readable to everyone

    Visual dish cards in English, Thai and Burmese, with a live order summary that updates as the meal is built.

  3. Give the owner one control room

    A real-time dashboard with six live charts, revenue and the pending queue. Menu, sell-out and promotion editing, order history, driver accounts and a single shop open/close toggle.

  4. Add AI where it saves time

    Gemini generates a plain-language daily sales summary (top dishes, peak hours, suggestions) and reads each payment slip. Doubtful slips go to a Payment Review queue.

  5. Connect the last mile

    A driver app with a delivery queue, automatic dispatch to the nearest available driver, live GPS route and ETA, and push notifications for new assignments.

  6. Keep everything in sync

    One Next.js platform for three experiences, with Supabase Realtime keeping customer, admin and driver views synchronised.

§ 04

The results

3connected apps: customer, admin and driver
3menu languages: English, Thai, Burmese
7taps from browsing to a paid, tracked order

The system is built and live. Customers order and pay from a QR code, the owner runs the day from one dashboard, and drivers get routed deliveries. AI daily sales insights and AI payment-slip verification are both working. Smart restock alerts are planned next.

For the shop, the biggest change is that orders are now structured data instead of chat messages. That means every order is confirmed, every delivery is visible, and the owner can finally see what sells and when.
