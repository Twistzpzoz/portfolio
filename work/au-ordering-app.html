Case study 05Product design · UI/UX

AU Wallet

A campus digital wallet for ordering and paying at Assumption University, designed to be quick enough to use while standing in a queue.

My role
UI/UX designer: screens, prototype & testing
Team
Team Nexus · 5 members
Context
Assumption University
Tools
Figma, moderated user testing
§ 01

The problem

At lunchtime the campus cafeteria means long queues, and students make small payments there every day.

We set out to answer one question: can an app solve the cafeteria queue problem and make paying for daily expenses easy? The core job was ordering and payment: browse menus, place an order and pay, either in person at the cafeteria or online.

§ 02

My role

I was a UI/UX designer on a five-person team. I designed the ordering and payment screens and built the interactive prototypes in Figma.

I also helped run the user testing with students, analyse their feedback and turn it into the next round of designs.

§ 03

The process

  1. Design the core job

    Ordering and payment in one flow: browse menus, order and pay at the cafeteria or online.

  2. Add the everyday extras

    Menu browsing, wallet balance and transaction history, and earning reward points.

  3. Prototype in Figma

    A clickable Figma prototype, shared with a QR code so anyone can try it.

  4. Test one student at a time

    We explained the app, let each participant use it without help, recorded how they navigated, then asked four questions on ease of use, missing features, whether it solved the queue problem, and suggestions.

  5. Iterate

    We looked for patterns in the feedback and redesigned the screens that caused the most friction.

§ 04

The results

14students tested, from 4 different faculties
12 / 14said the app solves the queue problem and makes payment easy (2 neutral, 0 no)
~4.4 minaverage test session
“Smooth navigation, everything was where I expected.”

What testers struggled with

  • Home versus ordering: two testers confused the home page with the food-order page, and another asked for a clearer order page.
  • History: three couldn't find past orders, receipts or transaction history.
  • Settings: two took time to find them.
  • Requests: a menu search bar, push notifications, saved favourites, a first-time tutorial and dark mode.

What we changed

  • A wallet-first home screen. The home page used to open on a shop list that looked like the ordering page. It now shows the balance up front, with one-tap Top up, Points and History, plus the pre-order list and offers.
  • A real scanner for in-person payment. The static QR screen became a live camera scanner for the cafeteria's payment QR, followed by a transfer screen with the amount, a category and a points discount.
