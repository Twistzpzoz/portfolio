Case study 01Startup · Market research & marketing

RentSync

“Airbnb for gadgets.” A peer-to-peer platform that lets students and freelancers rent tech from people who already own it, for days instead of buying.

My role
Team lead (CEO): vision, research & marketing plan
Team
Team Peak · 5 members
Context
Final startup pitch, Assumption University
Methods
Market sizing, survey, usability tests, interviews
§ 01

The problem

A MacBook Pro costs more than 50,000 THB, but most students only need one for a project that lasts a few weeks. Borrowing from friends is awkward and unreliable, with no guarantee of availability, condition or accountability.

Meanwhile, the people who do own expensive gear leave it sitting idle. In our research, 70% of gadget owners said their devices go unused for three months or more each year, losing value while earning nothing.

Most people don't need to own a camera. They need one for Saturday.

Who feels it

  • University students (about 1.55 million in Thailand): a laptop or camera for one project, on a short deadline.
  • Creators and freelancers (about 7.4 million active in Thailand): different gear for every shoot.
  • Professionals: presentation kit for an event, without travelling with expensive devices.
  • Device owners: idle gadgets and no trusted way to rent them out.
§ 02

My role

I was the team's CEO, leading the overall vision and business direction of a five-person team, with teammates as COO, CMO, CFO and CTO.

  • Worked on market sizing (TAM, SAM, SOM), competitor research, customer personas and the value proposition.
  • Helped design and run customer validation with 70 participants across three methods.
  • Built audience segments and the digital go-to-market plan for students, freelancers and professionals.
  • Co-presented the investor-style final pitch.
§ 03

The process

  1. Size the market

    Global electronics rental is a $29.1B market (TAM), $325.6M in Southeast Asia (SAM). Our obtainable share is $3.5M: 1% of SEA capture among Thai urban tech renters by year three (SOM).

  2. Benchmark the alternatives

    Compared RentSync with Facebook Marketplace, rental shops and buying new on seven features: verified identity, digital agreements, secure deposits, in-app messaging, ratings, short-term flexibility and cost.

  3. Design a model built on trust

    Government-ID verification, a security deposit held in escrow, an auto-generated rental agreement per booking, mutual ratings and a full transaction log, because a stranger is lending you a 50,000 THB laptop.

  4. Make it pay

    A 12% commission on every rental is the main revenue driver, plus 150 THB featured listings for seven days and an optional 5% insurance add-on.

  5. Prototype the core flow

    A responsive web MVP covering Browse → Book → Agree → Pay → Review, with separate renter and owner journeys of ten steps each.

  6. Plan the launch

    A TOFU/MOFU/BOFU funnel: campus posters with QR codes at Assumption, Mahidol and Chula, trust-building content, a 50 THB first-rental credit and a 100 THB referral reward.

§ 04

The results

We tested the prototype and the business model assumptions with 70 people across three methods: an online survey (n = 50), moderated usability tests (n = 12) and interviews with renters and owners (n = 8).

84%of survey respondents would use RentSync to rent a device
90%completed the booking flow in usability testing
4.2/5average ease-of-use rating
76%own a gadget they would consider listing
5/5interviewed renters said the deposit system builds trust
14months to projected break-even, early in year two

The biggest insight came from the interviews: ID verification and the deposit system were the top trust signals for both renters and owners. That confirmed trust and safety as the core of the product, not an extra feature.

The three-year projection grows commission revenue from 129,600 THB to 972,000 THB as active renters grow from 200 to 1,500.
